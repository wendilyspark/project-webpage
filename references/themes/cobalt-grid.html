NOTE 02 · BriefSheet 03 / 16

Located in a rare prime inner-city plot with school-district advantages and elite community resources, the project faced multiple simultaneous challenges: policy restrictions on height limits, density caps, demolition bands resulting in irregular lot shapes, and unconventional unit layouts.

Site-level factors affecting ultra-luxury value perception and investment return pressures meant the client could not price from precedent. We were commissioned to conduct deep ultra-luxury market research and consumer insights — clarifying target buyer profiles, product optimisation directions, and innovative commercial model exploration.

NOTE 07 · Core findingSheet 08 / 16

Brand contributes least to realised premium — and receives the largest share of ultra-luxury marketing spend.

Section 02 — DeliverableSheets 09–10

Four UHNW buyer segments

A three-dimensional assessment model — investment, residential and social-circle attributes — resolving a single "luxury buyer" into four addressable groups.

FIG. 08 · Segment profilesSheet 09 / 16
Segment 01

Legacy families

Multi-generational holders. Buy the address, not the building. Indifferent to brand; highly sensitive to neighbours, school access and long-run land scarcity.

Wealth accumulators

Treat the unit as a portfolio position. Liquidity and resale velocity dominate; design quality matters as an exit argument rather than a lifestyle preference.

Segment 03

Professional elite

C-suite and senior professionals. Prioritise comfort, privacy and circle integration. Extremely high bar on product detail: ceiling height, orientation, flow. Rational decision-making, value-conscious.

New money

Emerging wealth. Prioritise belonging and prestige. Most brand-sensitive segment: most influenced by marketing, most active word-of-mouth amplifiers. Strongest need for circle admission.
