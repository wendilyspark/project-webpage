Project Background

Located in a rare prime inner-city plot with school-district advantages and elite community resources, the project faced multiple simultaneous challenges: policy restrictions on height limits, density caps, demolition bands resulting in irregular lot shapes, and unconventional unit layouts. Site-level factors affecting ultra-luxury value perception and investment return pressures meant the client could not price from precedent. We were commissioned to conduct deep ultra-luxury market research and consumer insights — clarifying target buyer profiles, product optimisation directions, and innovative commercial model exploration.

Brand contributes least to realised premium — and receives the largest share of ultra-luxury marketing spend.

Reallocating that spend toward location narrative and architectural differentiation is the highest-leverage move available before launch. Brand: 24% of realised premium, 61% of marketing spend — ranks 4th of four drivers.

DELIVERABLE 01

Four UHNW Buyer Segments & Value Framework

A three-dimensional assessment model — investment, residential and social-circle attributes — resolving a single "luxury buyer" into four addressable groups.

Legacy Families

Multi-generational holders. Buy the address, not the building. Indifferent to brand; highly sensitive to neighbours, school access and long-run land scarcity.

Wealth Accumulators

Treat the unit as a portfolio position. Liquidity and resale velocity dominate; design quality matters as an exit argument rather than a lifestyle preference.

Professional Elite

C-suite and senior professionals. Prioritise comfort, privacy and circle integration. Extremely high bar on product detail: ceiling height, orientation, flow. Rational decision-making, value-conscious.

New Money

Emerging wealth. Prioritise belonging and prestige. Most brand-sensitive segment: most influenced by marketing, most active word-of-mouth amplifiers. Strongest need for circle admission.
