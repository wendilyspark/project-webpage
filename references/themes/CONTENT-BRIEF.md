## 02 — Deliverable: Four UHNW buyer segments
Heading: **Four UHNW buyer segments**
Standfirst: *A three-dimensional assessment model — investment, residential and social-circle attributes — resolving a single "luxury buyer" into four addressable groups.*

- **Segment 01 · Legacy families** — Multi-generational holders. Buy the address, not the building. Indifferent to brand; highly sensitive to neighbours, school access and long-run land scarcity. — Scarcity 96, Privacy 78, Brand 12
- **Segment 02 · Wealth accumulators** — Treat the unit as a portfolio position. Liquidity and resale velocity dominate; design quality matters as an exit argument rather than a lifestyle preference. — Liquidity 92, Prestige 60, Comfort 34
- **Segment 03 · Professional elite** — C-suite and senior professionals. Prioritise comfort, privacy and circle integration. Extremely high bar on product detail: ceiling height, orientation, flow. Rational decision-making, value-conscious. — Comfort 94, Privacy 88, Brand 26
- **Segment 04 · New money** — Emerging wealth. Prioritise belonging and prestige. Most brand-sensitive segment: most influenced by marketing, most active word-of-mouth amplifiers. Strongest need for circle admission. — Belonging 90, Brand 84, Scarcity 40

### Segment map — motive against price elasticity (2x2)
X axis *Purchase motive: use → asset*; Y axis *Price elasticity*.
- Low elasticity / use → **Professional elite** — Will pay for resolved product detail. Walks away from compromise.
- Low elasticity / asset → **Legacy families** — Primary target. Price-insensitive where scarcity is genuine. *(this quadrant is highlighted — the data supports it)*
- High elasticity / use → **New money** — Responsive to narrative and brand. Needs the circle to be legible.
- High elasticity / asset → **Wealth accumulators** — Buys on exit maths. Will trade design for liquidity.

## 03 — Deliverable: Eight value dimensions
Heading: **Eight value dimensions**
Standfirst: *A product-optimisation framework translating segment psychology into decisions the design and sales teams could act on.*

| Dimension | What it governs | Primary segment | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scarcity | Irreproducibility of location, architecture and unit type. Legacy families rely on it almost exclusively. | Legacy families | 5 |
| Value preservation | Historical price trajectory and future liquidity. School-district anchor is the strongest single support. | Legacy families | 4 |
| Liquidity | Market transaction depth. Ultra-luxury segment liquidity is thin by design — a key concern for investor-type buyers. | Wealth accumulators | 4 |
| Comfort | Layout, finish standards, property services. Policy-constrained irregular layouts require design mitigation. | Professional elite | 3 |
| Privacy | Security systems, community density, resident-circle homogeneity. | Professional elite | 3 |
| Prestige | Entry immediacy, exclusive service details, visible service standards. | New money | 3 |
| Belonging | Circle community resources, resident events, admission mechanics. | New money | 2 |
| Purity | Pure residential with no commercial interference — the top hidden priority for legacy and professional segments. | Cross-segment | 4 |

### Pull quote
**"Purity ranked above prestige in every segment — and no benchmark project was designing for it."** — *Finding · Depth interviews*

### Three response layers
- **Layer 01 · Product — Layout remediation programme** — Forty-plus specific moves converting policy-forced irregularity into differentiated unit typologies rather than compromised ones.
- **Layer 02 · Service — Circle admission design** — Resident-community mechanics that make belonging legible to new-money buyers without diluting the homogeneity legacy families are paying for.
- **Layer 03 · Narrative — Scarcity story architecture** — A sales narrative sequenced by segment, leading with location scarcity and treating brand as supporting evidence rather than the headline.
