00 Context

A flagship asset with no obvious comparable#

Four simultaneous constraints meant the client could not price from precedent.

  • Constraint 01

    Height limits

    Policy restrictions cap buildable height, compressing the achievable unit mix.

  • Constraint 02

    Density caps

    Plot ratio ceilings limit saleable area against a very high land basis.

  • Constraint 03

    Demolition bands

    Phased clearance leaves irregular lot boundaries that resist orthogonal planning.

  • Constraint 04

    Unconventional layouts

    Irregular footprints force non-standard floorplans that UHNW buyers scrutinise.

Located in a rare prime inner-city plot with school-district advantages and elite community resources, the project faced multiple simultaneous challenges: policy restrictions on height limits, density caps, demolition bands resulting in irregular lot shapes, and unconventional unit layouts.

Site-level factors affecting ultra-luxury value perception and investment return pressures meant the client could not price from precedent. We were commissioned to conduct deep ultra-luxury market research and consumer insights — clarifying target buyer profiles, product optimisation directions, and innovative commercial model exploration.

01 Benchmark analysis

How comparable assets manufacture scarcity#

Eight ultra-luxury projects, decomposed by the source of their premium rather than by their marketing claim.

A

A · Collector-oriented

Art & scarcity positioning

Art collection as an extreme scarcity anchor. Ultra-exclusive clubhouse, buyers under 1,000 UHNW families, invitation-only release cycles.

B

B · Cultural-form

Chinese courtyard heritage

Courtyard compound design benchmark. Chinese garden plus private courtyard plus multi-space differentiation; buyers are local landowning legacy families.

C

C · Positional

Absolute location scarcity

Prime location address, pricing and liquidity accepted as the purest embodiment of "location = value". Resale velocity materially above segment average.

D

D · Narrative

Heritage & cultural narrative

Inside a historic conservation district. Historical depth plus cultural-circle premium validates cultural storytelling support for ultra-luxury pricing.

Premium source hierarchy#

Finding

Value perception varies significantly by buyer type. Legacy families are location-driven; wealth accumulators weight design and display; professional elites weight comfort and design; new money weights brand recognition.

The contradiction#

4th

Core finding

Brand contributes least to realised premium — and receives the largest share of ultra-luxury marketing spend.

Recommendation

Reallocating that spend toward location narrative and architectural differentiation is the highest-leverage move available before launch.

02 Deliverable

Four UHNW buyer segments#

Method

A three-dimensional assessment model — investment, residential and social-circle attributes — resolving a single "luxury buyer" into four addressable groups.

01 Segment 01

Legacy families

Multi-generational holders. Buy the address, not the building. Indifferent to brand; highly sensitive to neighbours, school access and long-run land scarcity.

  • Scarcity96
  • Privacy78
  • Brand12
02 Segment 02

Wealth accumulators

Treat the unit as a portfolio position. Liquidity and resale velocity dominate; design quality matters as an exit argument rather than a lifestyle preference.

  • Liquidity92
  • Prestige60
  • Comfort34
03 Segment 03

Professional elite

C-suite and senior professionals. Prioritise comfort, privacy and circle integration. Extremely high bar on product detail: ceiling height, orientation, flow. Rational decision-making, value-conscious.

  • Comfort94
  • Privacy88
  • Brand26
04 Segment 04

New money

Emerging wealth. Prioritise belonging and prestige. Most brand-sensitive segment: most influenced by marketing, most active word-of-mouth amplifiers. Strongest need for circle admission.

  • Belonging90
  • Brand84
  • Scarcity40

Eight value dimensions#

Method

A product-optimisation framework translating segment psychology into decisions the design and sales teams could act on.

Finding

“Purity ranked above prestige in every segment — and no benchmark project was designing for it.”

— Finding · Depth interviews

Three response layers#

  1. Layer 01 · Product

    Layout remediation programme

    Forty-plus specific moves converting policy-forced irregularity into differentiated unit typologies rather than compromised ones.

  2. Layer 02 · Service

    Circle admission design

    Resident-community mechanics that make belonging legible to new-money buyers without diluting the homogeneity legacy families are paying for.

  3. Layer 03 · Narrative

    Scarcity story architecture

    A sales narrative sequenced by segment, leading with location scarcity and treating brand as supporting evidence rather than the headline.
